# World tour art rules

- The national flag is oversized across the rink but softly dissolves into bright neutral ice, following the approved Istanbul treatment. Prefer restrained pale colours, broad frosted fades, and a large recognizable symbol over a dense uniform fill.
- Tricolours stay recognizable at full-rink scale, but their colours may diffuse naturally into the ice instead of forming hard edge-to-edge painted blocks.
- Flag artwork is independent of hockey markings. The canonical daily-court geometry and crisp markings are composited above it.
- Do not inherit buildings, pavilions, people, cars, benches, or other scene content from the geometry reference. Every background element must belong to the named city.
- Use a geographically plausible season and weather. Snow is only used where it is a natural part of the chosen city treatment.
- Keep ice bright enough for the puck, player, goalkeeper, goal, and hit feedback to remain legible.
- Preview compositions must vary by camera height, angle, goalkeeper placement, pose, and gaze while preserving one shared quality bar.
- Save sprites use the daily-game asymmetric butterfly mechanics: one pad seals the ice while the other remains slightly raised and angled.
- Goalie pads must preserve the same physical length, width, thickness, and overall proportions between the ready and save sprites. The save pose may change their angle and introduce natural perspective foreshortening, but it must never redesign, shorten, narrow, crop, or miniaturize either pad.
